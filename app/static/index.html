What does the score mean?

The fault score (0–1) is the network's output. Higher means the recording sounds more like the faulty recordings it was trained on. It is a ranking score, not a calibrated probability: a score of 0.97 does not mean a 97 % chance of a fault.

The alarm threshold was set on the validation machine so that about 5 % of its normal recordings raise an alarm. *The false-alarm rate shown is measured on that machine. On a different machine it can be very different – on the test machine it was up to 99.9 % for fans.

Seed agreement shows how many of the three independently trained copies of the final design would raise an alarm. Disagreement means the result is uncertain.

        How to read this – and its limits

        The heat map shows where in the recording the network found fault-like patterns. It does not show why the machine is faulty, and it does not prove that the highlighted sound is the real cause of the score. Grad-CAM is an approximation built from the network's last layer, so it is blurry: it works on a coarse grid of blocks (the grid size is shown below the map), not on single frequencies.

        The check hides the highlighted area (replaces it with an average level) and runs the network again. If the score falls more than when random areas are hidden, the model really depends on that area. A failed check means: do not trust the map for this recording.

        Validated means only that the model depends on the highlighted area (the check passed). Not validated means the map may look plausible but the model did not depend on it. Neither is a diagnosis of the physical fault.

        A clean map does not mean a healthy machine. Use the explanation to decide where to listen or look during an inspection, not to replace the inspection.

        Model limitations – please read

        • Not proven on new machines. On the held-out test machine the deployed model caught – of faults at a 5 % false-alarm budget, no better than chance. A simple loudness rule did better (–).
        • Thresholds must be calibrated per machine. Thresholds come from one validation machine (id_04). On another machine almost every clip may raise an alarm.
        • Narrow training data. Trained on 2 pumps and 2 fans from the MIMII dataset, 6 dB signal-to-noise ratio, microphone channel 0, 10-s clips.
        • Pumps and fans only. Other machines (valves, slide rails, motors) are outside its knowledge, even if the input checks pass.
        • Sensitive to recording conditions. Microphone position, gain and background noise change the sound; the checks above flag only large differences.
        • A screening aid, not a decision. Use it to prioritise inspections. Never skip a scheduled inspection because of a "no alarm" result.
